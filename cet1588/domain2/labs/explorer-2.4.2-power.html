Keep the lights on.

Network gear is useless without clean, reliable power, and "reliable" is engineered, not assumed. A UPS carries the load through a blink or a brief outage on battery; a PDU distributes that power to every device in the rack; and behind both sits arithmetic you can't fudge: the power load your equipment draws and the voltage it runs on must fit within your UPS rating and your circuit's amperage. Trace the power chain below, then use the calculator to size a real rack \u2014 and watch what happens to the amps when you change the voltage.

Objective 2.4 lists UPS, PDU, power load, and voltage under power. The exam expects you to know what each device does and the basic relationship watts = volts \u00d7 amps \u2014 enough to reason about whether a load fits a UPS and a circuit. You won't do heavy math on test day, but you must understand that a higher voltage carries the same wattage at lower current, and that UPS/PDU sizing is about not exceeding capacity. UPS vs generator — they solve different durations

A UPS reacts instantly but only lasts minutes (it's a battery). A generator takes seconds to start but runs for hours on fuel. In a serious facility they work together: the UPS carries the load through the gap until the generator spins up, so equipment never sees an interruption.

The 80% continuous-load rule

Both the UPS and the branch circuit should be loaded to no more than 80% of their rating for anything running continuously. A 20 A breaker therefore gives you 16 A of usable headroom, and a 2700 W UPS should carry roughly 2160 W of steady load. Sizing right to the limit leaves no margin for startup surges or growth \u2014 always leave the 20% cushion.

One-line memory hook

UPS = keeps power on (battery + protection). PDU = spreads power out (a managed power strip for the rack). They're often stacked: utility \u2192 UPS \u2192 PDU \u2192 devices.

Key terms
UPS
Uninterruptible Power Supply — battery system that rides through short outages and conditions power; bridges the gap until power returns or a generator starts.
PDU
Power Distribution Unit — distributes a power feed to multiple rack outlets; a managed power strip, often metered or switched.
Power load
The total wattage all equipment draws; you size UPS and circuits to stay safely under their limits.
Voltage
Electrical "pressure" (e.g., 120 V vs 208/240 V). For a given wattage, higher voltage means lower current (amps).
Watts = Volts × Amps
The core relationship. Watts measure load; amps measure current; volts is the supply pressure.
80% rule
Continuous loads should use no more than 80% of a circuit's or UPS's rating, leaving headroom for surges and growth.
