Where the network lives.

Before a single packet moves, the physical network has to live somewhere — in wiring closets and equipment rooms laid out to a deliberate hierarchy. The Main Distribution Frame (MDF) is the building's central nervous system, where the outside world connects in. Intermediate Distribution Frames (IDFs) are the floor-level closets that fan that connectivity out to the desks. Get this layout wrong and you get long cable runs, dead floors, and a maintenance nightmare. Click through the building below to see how it all fits together.

The one-sentence rule the exam wants

A building has exactly one MDF (the central hub where the ISP demarc and core equipment live) and many IDFs (floor- or zone-level closets that uplink to the MDF over backbone cabling). If you remember only that, you'll answer most 2.4 location questions correctly.

The two kinds of cabling

Structured cabling comes in two roles, and the distinction matters on the exam. Backbone cabling is the high-capacity vertical spine between closets; horizontal cabling is the runs from a closet out to the desks.

Backbone cabling
a.k.a. vertical cabling / riser

Connects the MDF to each IDF (and sometimes IDF-to-IDF). Carries aggregated traffic between closets, so it needs high capacity and often spans long vertical distances between floors.

Usually: fiber optic (distance + bandwidth)
Horizontal cabling
closet-to-work-area

Runs from an IDF out to the work-area outlets on that floor. This is the cabling that reaches individual desks, phones, and access points.

Usually: copper (Cat 6 / 6A) · ~90 m permanent link, 100 m channel

Key terms
MDF
Main Distribution Frame — the building's single central equipment room where the WAN/demarc terminates and core equipment lives.
IDF
Intermediate Distribution Frame — a floor- or zone-level wiring closet that aggregates local cabling and uplinks to the MDF.
Demarcation point
The boundary where the service provider's responsibility ends and the customer's network begins.
Backbone cabling
Vertical/riser cabling connecting the MDF to IDFs (and IDF-to-IDF); typically fiber for distance and capacity.
Horizontal cabling
Cabling from an IDF to work-area outlets; typically Cat 6/6A copper within the ~100 m channel limit.
Cross-connect
The patch-panel point in an MDF/IDF where cabling is interconnected and managed.
