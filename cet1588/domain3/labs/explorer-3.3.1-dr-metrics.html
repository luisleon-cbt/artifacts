How much, how fast.

When disaster strikes, two questions decide how badly it hurts: how much data did we lose? and how long until we're back? Those map to the two metrics the exam tests hardest , RPO (Recovery Point Objective) looks backward to your last good backup, and RTO (Recovery Time Objective) looks forward to the moment service returns. They're constantly confused. Drag the disaster and the recovery on the timeline below and watch both numbers move , then meet the reliability metrics (MTTR, MTBF, MTTF) that round out the picture.

Network+ Context — Read this first

Objective 3.3 expects you to define and distinguish these metrics. The two that get confused on every exam are RPO vs RTO: RPO is about data (how far back your last recovery point is , how much data you can afford to lose), and RTO is about time (how long recovery may take). The reliability trio , MTTR (mean time to repair), MTBF (mean time between failures), and MTTF (mean time to failure) , measure how often things break and how quickly they're fixed. Lower is better for RPO/RTO/MTTR; higher is better for MTBF/MTTF.

The trick to never confusing them again

RPO = Point (a point in the past , your last good backup; measures data loss). RTO = Time (the duration to recover; measures downtime). Want a smaller RPO? Back up more often. Want a smaller RTO? Recover faster (warmer site, automation, rehearsed plan). Both are targets the business sets , "objectives" , not guarantees.

MTBF vs MTTF — the one-line difference

MTBF is for repairable things (it includes the time between one failure and the next, repairs and all). MTTF is for things you replace rather than repair (it measures expected lifespan until that one failure). Both describe how long something runs; higher is better for each.

Key terms
RPO
Recovery Point Objective — the maximum acceptable data loss, measured backward to the last good recovery point. Smaller = back up more often.
RTO
Recovery Time Objective — the maximum acceptable time to restore service after a disaster. Smaller = recover faster.
MTTR
Mean Time To Repair — the average time to fix a failed system and return it to service. Lower is better.
MTBF
Mean Time Between Failures — for repairable systems, the average operating time between one failure and the next. Higher is better.
MTTF
Mean Time To Failure — for non-repairable (replaced) items, the average lifespan until failure. Higher is better.
Objective
RPO and RTO are business-set targets, not guarantees — they drive how often you back up and how fast your recovery design must be.
