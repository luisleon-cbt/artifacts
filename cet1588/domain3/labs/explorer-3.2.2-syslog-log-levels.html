Eight levels of "how bad?"

When a device sends a syslog message, it stamps each one with a severity level from 0 to 7 , and here's the catch students always trip on: lower numbers are more severe. Level 0 (Emergency) means the system is falling over; level 7 (Debug) is chatty diagnostic noise. Setting a logging threshold tells a device "send me everything this severe or worse." Click through the eight levels below, then drag the threshold and watch a live console filter its messages in real time.

Network+ Context — Read this first

The syslog severity scale (0\u20137) is a classic exam item. You must know that 0 = Emergency is the most severe and 7 = Debug is the least (and the most verbose), plus the order in between. You also need the threshold rule: configuring a logging level of N captures every message from 0 through N and filters the rest. Get the direction right , a lower number is more serious , and most syslog questions fall into place.

Why production rarely runs at Debug (7)

Level 7 Debug captures everything, including high-volume diagnostic chatter , useful while troubleshooting, but it can flood a syslog server and bury the messages that matter. Production devices typically log at a lower, less verbose level (often around Notice or Informational) and only crank up to Debug temporarily when chasing a specific problem.

Key terms
Severity level (0\u20137)
The seriousness stamped on each syslog message. Lower is more severe: 0 = Emergency, 7 = Debug.
Emergency (0)
The most severe level , the system is unusable. Demands immediate attention.
Debug (7)
The least severe and most verbose level , detailed diagnostic output used while troubleshooting.
Logging threshold
The configured level N. The device captures every message from severity 0 up through N and filters the rest.
"Lower = more severe"
The rule that trips people up: a smaller severity number means a more serious condition.
Log review
Regularly examining collected logs (often centralized on a syslog server) to spot problems, security events, and trends.
