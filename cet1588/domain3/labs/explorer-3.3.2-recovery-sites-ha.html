Somewhere to fail over to.

If your data center floods, where does the business run? The answer is a recovery site , and they come in three temperatures. A cold site is cheap but empty; a hot site is a fully running duplicate ready in minutes; a warm site sits in between. The hotter the site, the faster you recover , and the more it costs. The same trade-off shapes high availability: do two systems share the load (active-active), or does one wait on standby (active-passive)? Switch between them below.

Network+ Context — Read this first

Objective 3.3 expects you to rank the three recovery sites by readiness, cost, and recovery speed: cold = cheapest but slowest (just space/power), hot = priciest but near-instant (a live duplicate), warm = the middle. Tie this to 3.3.1: a hotter site gives a lower RTO. You also need active-active vs active-passive: in active-active both nodes serve traffic; in active-passive a standby waits to take over on failover.

The trade-off in one line

As a site gets warmer (cold → warm → hot), cost rises and recovery time falls. You're buying down your RTO with money: a hot site recovers in minutes but costs the most to maintain; a cold site is nearly free to keep but takes days to bring up. The right choice depends on how costly downtime is for the business.

High availability — two ways to stay up

Share the load, or wait in the wings.

High availability keeps a service running through a failure by having more than one node. The two designs differ in what the second node does day-to-day. Toggle between them.

Active-active vs active-passive — the quick contrast

Active-active: every node works all the time, sharing the load , better resource use and capacity, but every node must be maintained and sized for the shared load. Active-passive: one node works while the other waits idle and takes over on failover , simpler, but you're paying for standby capacity that sits unused most of the time.

Key terms
Cold site
Space with power and cooling but no installed hardware or data. Cheapest to keep; slowest (days) to bring online.
Warm site
Hardware and connectivity pre-installed, data synced periodically. Moderate cost; recovery in hours. The common compromise.
Hot site
A fully running duplicate with near-real-time data and near-immediate failover. Highest cost; lowest RTO (minutes).
Cost vs recovery trade-off
Warmer sites cost more but recover faster. You buy down RTO with money; the right level depends on downtime cost.
Active-active
All nodes serve traffic simultaneously, sharing the load. Better resource use; every node must carry the shared load if one fails.
Active-passive
One node serves while a standby waits idle and takes over on failover. Simpler, but standby capacity sits unused day-to-day.
