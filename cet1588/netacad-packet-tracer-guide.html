01
Create your NetAcad accountCisco Networking Academy · cisco.com
01

Open the NetAcad sign-up page

In your browser go to netacad.com and click Sign Up (top-right corner). Cisco Networking Academy is free and open to everyone — you don't need a CBT-issued course code for the Packet Tracer download itself.

Use your CBT student email (the one ending in your CBT domain). It will keep your NetAcad transcript tied to your CBT enrollment if your instructor ever needs to verify completion.
02

Fill in your details & confirm email

Enter your name, email, country (United States), and a strong password. Cisco emails you a confirmation link — open your inbox and click it. Without confirmation you can't enroll in courses.

If the email doesn't arrive in 5 minutes, check your spam folder. Add do-not-reply@netacad.com to your safe sender list.
03

Sign in and reach the Resource Hub

Once your email is confirmed, log in at netacad.com. From any page on the site, navigate to the Resource Hub at netacad.com/resources/lab-downloads. This is your one-stop page for Packet Tracer, virtual machines, and other lab files.

02
Download & install Packet TracerVersion 9.0 · ≈ 250 MB
04

Pick the installer for your operating system

On the Resource Hub, scroll to the Cisco Packet Tracer section. You'll see download buttons for each platform. Click the one matching your computer:

  • Windows — CiscoPacketTracer_900_Windows_64bit.exe
  • macOS — CiscoPacketTracer_900_macOS.dmg (works on Intel & Apple Silicon)
  • Linux (Ubuntu/Debian) — CiscoPacketTracer_900_Ubuntu_64bit.deb
Packet Tracer is not available for iPad, iPhone, Android, or Chromebooks. You need a Windows PC, Mac, or Linux machine.
05

Run the installer

Windows:

  • Double-click CiscoPacketTracer_900_Windows_64bit.exe in your Downloads folder.
  • Accept the License Agreement → Next.
  • Accept the default destination folder (C:\Program Files\Cisco Packet Tracer) → Next.
  • Accept the default Start Menu folder → Next.
  • Choose your shortcut options (Desktop icon recommended) → Next.
  • Click Install. Windows may ask for administrator permission — say Yes.

macOS:

  • Double-click CiscoPacketTracer_900_macOS.dmg to mount the disk image.
  • Click Open if macOS warns it was downloaded from the internet.
  • Click Continue on the welcome screen, then accept the License Agreement.
  • Click Install — you'll be prompted for your Mac account password or Touch ID.
  • When the Create Maintenance Tool window appears, click Continue, then Done.
If macOS blocks the installer with "cannot be opened because Apple cannot check it": open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway next to the Packet Tracer entry.

Ubuntu / Debian Linux: Packet Tracer installs from the terminal. Open a terminal and navigate to where you saved the .deb file (typically ~/Downloads):

  • cd ~/Downloads
  • sudo apt-get install ./CiscoPacketTracer_900_Ubuntu_64bit.deb
  • Enter your user password when prompted.
  • On the License Agreement screen, use Tab to highlight <OK> and press Enter.
  • Tab to <Yes> and press Enter to accept the agreement.
The mouse will not work during the License Agreement prompt. Use Tab to navigate and Enter to confirm. Once the install finishes, launch Packet Tracer from your Applications menu or by running packettracer in a terminal.
06

Finish the installer and launch

When the installer completes, leave the Launch Cisco Packet Tracer checkbox ticked and click Finish. On macOS or Linux, open Packet Tracer from your Applications folder or Activities menu.

The first time you launch, you'll see a prompt asking "Would you like to run multi-user when the application starts?" — click No for now (multi-user is for advanced shared labs).

Windows Security Alert? Check both Private networks and Public networks and click Allow Access. Packet Tracer needs network access for the multi-user feature later in the course.
03
Activate with your NetAcad loginOne-time linking · keeps your install authorized
07

Sign in from the Packet Tracer login window

Packet Tracer opens to a Cisco Packet Tracer Login window. You'll see a "Keep me logged in (for 3 months)" toggle — turn it on if you're on your personal computer, leave it off on shared or lab computers.

Click the big LOGIN button. Packet Tracer will open a browser tab to authenticate against NetAcad.

08

Authorize the browser tab

One of two things happens in the browser:

  • If you're still logged in to netacad.com — you'll see "You have successfully logged in to Cisco Packet Tracer. You may close this tab." Close it and return to Packet Tracer.
  • If your session has expired — sign back in to NetAcad with the credentials you created in Phase 1. Same success message appears. Close the tab.
Packet Tracer authenticates against your NetAcad account every 3 months (or every launch if "Keep me logged in" is off). Just keep your NetAcad password handy.
04
Verify your installTwo quick checks before lab day
09

Confirm the version

In Packet Tracer's menu bar, click Help → About. You should see Cisco Packet Tracer 9.0.0 (build numbers may vary). If you see an older version (8.x), the installer was outdated — re-download from the Resource Hub.

10

Drop a router and switch on the canvas

Try a quick smoke test:

  • In the bottom-left device tray, click the Routers icon and drag a 2911 router onto the workspace.
  • Click the Switches icon and drag a 2960 switch next to it.
  • Click the Connections (lightning bolt) icon, choose the straight copper cable, and connect GigabitEthernet0/0 on the router to GigabitEthernet0/1 on the switch.

If the devices appear and you can connect them, you're ready for the first lab.

Save the file as cbt-smoke-test.pkt — it's your first piece of evidence that the environment is working. Keep it; instructors sometimes ask to see it on day one.

You're ready for lab day.

NetAcad account created, Packet Tracer 9.0 installed and authorized, smoke test passed. Open your Canvas course and head to the first networking module to begin.
