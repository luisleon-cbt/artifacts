The layer model, and everything that hangs off it

Four modules. Work through them in order, then come back to the ones that did not stick.

The seven layers

Click any layer. The exam rarely asks you to recite the list; it asks you to place a symptom, a device, or a protocol at the right layer. Read each one for what it decides, not just what it is called.

Encapsulation, one header at a time

Data does not travel as data. Each layer wraps what it receives from above and hands it down. The receiving host unwraps the same layers in reverse. Watch the wrappers appear, then watch them come off.

The part people get wrong: when a packet crosses a router, the source and destination IP addresses stay the same the whole way. The Ethernet frame is torn off and rebuilt with new MAC addresses at every single hop. IP is the trip; the frame is the taxi you change at each corner.

Appliances, placed at the layer they read

Two questions identify any device on the exam. What decision does it make, and what information does it read to make that decision? The second answer gives you the layer every time.

Four ways to address traffic

Same topology, four delivery models. Watch who receives the traffic and who does not, because that distinction is exactly what the exam tests.
